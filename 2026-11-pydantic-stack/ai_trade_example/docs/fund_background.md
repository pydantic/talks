# Situational Awareness LP: background

## The manager

Leopold Aschenbrenner was born in Germany in 2001 or 2002 and graduated from Columbia University in 2021 as
valedictorian, aged 19, with a degree in economics and mathematics-statistics. He worked on the FTX Future
Fund from February 2022 until the day of FTX's bankruptcy in November 2022, then joined OpenAI's
Superalignment team in 2023, co-authoring the "Weak to Strong Generalization" paper. OpenAI fired him in
April 2024 over an alleged leak of what he describes as a benign brainstorming document; he says the real
trigger was a memo to the board warning about industrial espionage and weak security.

In June 2024 he published "Situational Awareness: The Decade Ahead", a 165-page essay arguing that AI
systems would be able to do their own AI research by 2027, that this could compress a decade of progress
into under a year, and that the United States needed to treat AI as a national-security race with China.
The essay was widely read in Silicon Valley and gave the fund its name.

## The fund

- Founded in 2024, after the essay, with backing from Patrick and John Collison, Daniel Gross and Nat
  Friedman.
- Starting capital is reported inconsistently: about $225 million (Seeking Alpha, via the substack that
  covered the fund) or about $5 million (SpotGamma). Either way it was small.
- Strategy: leveraged, concentrated bets on the AI build-out, public and private. Long the picks and
  shovels of AI infrastructure (memory, neoclouds, data-centre power, bitcoin miners converting to AI
  hosting), short software companies the manager believed AI agents would hollow out. The fund also held
  private stakes, including a stake in Anthropic reported at roughly $5 billion.
- A very small team: coverage describes "a handful of employees".

## Returns and size

- Reported net return through 30 June 2026: about 439% for the first half of the year, with even larger
  gains since inception. Wikipedia cites roughly 1,000% since inception as of July 2026.
- Assets: over $20 billion by mid-2026 (Seeking Alpha), an estimated $24 billion (SpotGamma). Gross market
  exposure, including borrowing, is the figure behind the widely quoted "$45 billion" peak in early July
  2026. The Financial Times put public-equity holdings at roughly $16 billion at the time of the sale.
- The fund had drawn criticism before the collapse over the manager's lack of money-management experience
  and his FTX history.

## What went wrong, in one paragraph

A concentrated thematic long book on roughly 4x gross leverage, paired with software shorts, met a month in
which the longs fell 27 to 54 percent and the shorts rallied. Analysts quoted afterwards made the same
point: the outcome had little to do with whether the AI thesis is eventually right. The fund could not
survive a 30 percent drawdown, and a forced seller facing an opportunistic buyer gets a bad price.
