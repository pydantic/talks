# The July 2026 AI selloff

## The run-up

The memory trade had been the most extreme corner of the AI boom. Peak twelve-month gains cited at the end
of July 2026: Micron about 657%, Sandisk over 2,700%, Western Digital about 571%, Seagate about 405%. The
story was high-bandwidth memory (HBM) for AI accelerators being "sold out years in advance", which pulled
capital into every memory and storage name. SK Hynix's US listing priced at $149 a share, opened at $170
and peaked near $195.

## What broke

- Memory: by 28 July Micron was down about 33% from its high, Sandisk about 50%, Western Digital about 42%,
  Seagate about 34%. SK Hynix's US shares fell below $137, 8% under the listing price and 30% below the
  post-debut high. Reasons cited: manufacturers had expanded HBM capacity aggressively, NAND and DRAM
  output kept rising, and the IPO of China's ChangXin Memory Technologies (CXMT) raised the prospect of
  more domestic Chinese capacity.
- Chips broadly: on 29 July CNBC reported chip stocks had shed more than $1 trillion. Nvidia was about 17%
  off its high and TSMC about 20%. Intel, which had been up roughly 270% year to date entering July, lost
  21% in seven trading days from around 8 July.
- Korea: the KOSPI fell about 29% in a month, with an 11% single-day drop, entering a bear market.
- Big Tech: from around 19 July investors sold the hyperscalers on the question of whether near-term revenue
  could justify the AI capital-spending run rate.

## Winners and losers

- A UBS basket of AI-spending beneficiaries (semiconductors included) trailed a basket of stocks seen as at
  risk of AI disruption by a record 42 percentage points. Software names that had been shorted as "AI
  losers" rallied.
- Meta was the clearest large-cap winner, rising about 8.8% on the idea of monetising surplus compute by
  selling AI infrastructure as a service.
- Analysts described the reversal as overdone by 29 July; the names hit hardest bounced 20 to 28% on 30 July
  once the forced selling ended (see `unwind_timeline.md`).
