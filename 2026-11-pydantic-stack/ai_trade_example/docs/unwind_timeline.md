# The unwind, day by day

Dates are from press reports; where an outlet gives a different day, both are noted.

- Early July: gross exposure at its peak of about $45 billion after the reported 439% first-half return.
- 8 July: semiconductor selloff spreading; Intel down 21% over seven sessions.
- 19 July: Big Tech sold on AI-capex scrutiny.
- 24 July: the fund writes to investors describing the drawdown as a buying opportunity.
- 28 July: "memory boom goes bust" coverage; Micron, SK Hynix, Sandisk down about 30% and still falling.
  Losses in Nebius and Sandisk in particular increase pressure from lenders.
- 29 July: chip stocks have shed more than $1 trillion. The fund starts talks with potential buyers, after
  first trying to raise fresh capital and offering assets directly to its own investors.
- 30 July: before the US open the entire public book, longs and shorts together, is sold in a single block
  trade to Citadel at a discount. Bloomberg reports the fund unwinding trades and seeking capital, then that
  assets have fallen to about $10 billion after liquidations. CNBC reports the fund has been forced to
  unwind all public stock positions. From about 3 pm ET the liquidation discount reprices out: Nebius
  +27.1%, IREN +26.5%, Bloom Energy +25.6%, Sandisk +24.6%, Riot +23.7%, CoreWeave +22.0%, CleanSpark and
  Applied Digital +21.4%, Core Scientific +20.7%. Names never in the fund moved too: Cipher Mining +28.3%,
  Hut 8 +22.5%, TeraWulf +18.6%. SPY +1.8%, QQQ +3.5%, equal-weight S&P flat; Vertiv +2.7%, Nvidia +1.9%,
  SMH +6.5%.
- 31 July: CNBC's account of the "$45 billion to fire sale" collapse. The rebound holds, with the hardest-hit
  names 18 to 22% above their lows in the premarket. The scope of what Citadel bought is described
  variously as "the bulk", "a substantial portion", "roughly $16 billion" or "the entire public-equities
  portfolio".
- After: the fund keeps its private holdings, including the Anthropic stake. Total losses are put at
  about $35 billion of gross assets; the widely quoted figure is that a first-half return of 439% became a
  67% loss for the year.
